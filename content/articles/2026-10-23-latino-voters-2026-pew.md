About 39 million Latinos are projected to be eligible to vote in the 2026 midterm elections, or 16% of the roughly 250 million eligible voters in the United States, according to [estimates published Sept. 10 by the Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/2026/09/10/the-u-s-electorates-changing-racial-and-ethnic-makeup/).

That number has grown by about 5.25 million since the 2022 midterms, an increase of 16%. Over the same period, the total number of eligible voters grew by about 8.45 million, or 3%, meaning Latinos accounted for more than half of the electorate's growth. Pew's projections are based on U.S. Census Bureau data, including the American Community Survey and the bureau's population estimates.

## Where Latino voters live

Using 2024 census data, Pew found that the largest Latino electorates are in California (8.7 million eligible voters, or 33% of the state's electorate), Texas (6.7 million, 32%), Florida (3.8 million, 23%), New York (2.2 million, 16%) and Arizona (1.4 million, 25%). New Mexico has the highest Latino share of any state, at 43%.

## How Latino voters see the election

A [separate Pew survey released Oct. 16](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/2026/10/16/how-latino-voters-are-experiencing-the-2026-midterm-elections/), conducted Sept. 28 to Oct. 4, 2026, included 774 Latino registered voters among 5,062 U.S. adults. Among Latino registered voters:

- 51% said they would vote for the Democratic candidate for the House in their district, 24% for the Republican, 7% for another candidate, and 18% were not sure.
- 24% approved of President Trump's handling of his job, down from 38% at the start of his second term.
- 70% said the Trump administration's policies have been harmful to Hispanics.
- The economy remained their top issue.

Engagement has risen most among Latino voters who back Democratic House candidates. In that group, 77% said it really matters which party wins control of Congress, up from 58% in 2022; 53% said they were extremely motivated to vote, up from 41%; and 45% said they had given a lot of thought to the election, up from 24%.

Survey results are estimates and are subject to sampling error; Pew publishes its full methodology and [topline questionnaire](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/10/RE_2026.10.16-Latino-Voters_TOPLINE.pdf) alongside the report. Pew uses the terms Latino and Hispanic interchangeably.
